Smart Middle Click Autoscroll

Middle-click scrollable content, then move the pointer to control speed. Move back to the anchor to pause. Click again or press Esc to stop.

Links, tabs, and controls keep their normal middle-click actions. Holding a keyboard modifier also preserves the native click.

Set up

Enable the app in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. This lets it identify controls and generate scrolling across apps. It processes input locally without saving page text or keystrokes.

Options

Open Options in Settings or click the ↕ menu-bar icon.

  • Enable or pause autoscroll.
  • Adjust scrolling speed and horizontal scrolling.
  • Enable Start at login to open the app when you sign in. Move it to Applications first.
  • Show in Dock keeps Settings easy to reach if the menu bar is crowded.
  • Show in Menu Bar controls whether the ↕ icon is visible.
  • Keep native middle click in apps that need their own middle-button gestures.

Closing Settings leaves the app running in the background. Use Quit in Options or the menu bar to stop it.

You can hide both icons. To reopen Settings, open Smart Middle Click Autoscroll from Spotlight or Finder. Your visibility choices stay unchanged. A manual launch opens Settings; startup at login stays in the background when Accessibility is available.

If startup needs approval, click Login Items… in Settings. If the menu-bar icon is obscured, use Options or choose Show Menu Bar Icon from the app menu.

If scrolling does not start

Some apps do not expose enough Accessibility information. Smart Middle Click Autoscroll preserves the native click when it cannot identify a suitable target.

Check Diagnostics in Options or the menu bar for the last routing decision, or retry the input listener. After replacing or rebuilding the app, macOS may require removing and re-adding its Accessibility entry.

If you use a mouse remapper, it must deliver a middle click to the app. Avoid running multiple autoscroll utilities at once.
